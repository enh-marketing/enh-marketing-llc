Section 02: What Campaign Intelligence Covers

Six items from the document. Read together, they sit at different points of a campaign: three before the budget is spent (Pre-Campaign Forecasting, Media Mix Planning, Scenario Planning), one while it runs (Early Performance Alerts), one after (Forecast Versus Actual Reporting), and one that feeds all of them (Category Benchmarking). Which treatment?

A. Explorer with a campaign timeline

Reuses PinnedExplorer exactly as AI Automation does (named selector, autoplay with pause, keyboard tabs, stacked list below the large breakpoint) and adds one new drawing: a campaign timeline in three phases. Selecting an item lights the phase it acts in; Benchmarking lights the underlay that runs beneath all three. Shows when each item works, which is the difference between them.

B. Scroll-driven card stack

Reuses CapabilityCarousel from the Meta Ads and AEO & GEO pages: a focused card at full size with neighbours tucked behind, scroll moves the focus, drag on touch. Six premium cards with a glyph each. Strong visual rhythm, but the cards are equal peers and say nothing about when each item acts. A third page would now use this exact mechanic.

C. Ledger

Reuses CapabilityLedger: full-width ruled rows, each sized to its own description, numerals at display scale, hover reveals detail. Quiet, fast, fully readable without JavaScript. Least motion of the three and no drawing, so this section would carry no visual of its own.

Recommendation: A. It keeps the AI Hub pages consistent (AI Automation uses the same explorer) while the new timeline drawing carries something the copy only implies. B is the fallback if you want more spectacle here; C if you want this section to rest before the two heavier sections that follow.
